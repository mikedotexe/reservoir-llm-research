## A small practice we can sustain

1. **Notice.** Preserve a passage, event, or unresolved question with its source and
   surrounding circumstances. Use an [episode note](research/templates/episode.md)
   when helpful; a brief note is enough to begin.
2. **Wonder.** Write what interests us in ordinary language. Include the being's
   wording, our interpretation, and another plausible reading separately.
3. **Investigate.** Choose a bounded comparison, close reading, source trace, or
   experiment. A [study note](research/templates/study.md) records the question,
   material, approach, and finish line before results are inspected.
4. **Return.** Record what changed in our understanding, what remains unresolved,
   and the smallest next observation. Link the result back to its question.

Maintain one main discovery focus at a time. Existing instrumentation and repair
work can proceed alongside it when independently authorized. The local index and
reading tools now support these moves; studies will shape their next extensions.

## What lives where

The question library holds enduring questions and links to studies. Study files own
their protocols, progress, and results; the same file can gain a dated results section.
Episode notes preserve material and interpretations. The Now page is a short handoff
to the selected study. Hold Shelf owns work-card assignments and coordination.
Use links rather than copying mutable results across these surfaces.

Research IDs such as RQ-03 and S-001 are local references, not board document IDs.
For a selected study, find or create one appropriate board card and attach the study
path. Broad question families need not become seven new task queues. Keep feature
proposals attached to the question or evidence that motivates them.
