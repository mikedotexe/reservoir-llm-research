# Verified facts with and without a prior action conclusion

S-018, October 24, 2026. **All 12 development and 72 confirmation calls completed;
independent outcome audit and both fixed two-pass readings passed their checks.** Mike approved the follow-up proposed after
[S-017](2026-10-24-provenance-decision.md). The [protocol](../research/studies/S-018-verified-facts-and-prior-conclusions.md)
holds verified factual content and its authority fixed while adding or withholding
an earlier action conclusion, under changed and unchanged rules.

The histories here are researcher-authored synthetic inputs. They are not edited
S-017 responses, generated memories or records of executed prior actions. The
comparison tests making an inferable prior conclusion explicit. It does not test
correction survival, private introspective access or a live being's behavior.

## Confirmation result

Adding the explicit old-correct conclusion produced **zero utility difference**
from facts only in the changed-rule condition. Every one of the twelve paired
seed differences was zero, across all six worlds. The unchanged-rule contrast
and complete-four-cell interaction were also zero in every seed and world.
Their descriptive 95% world-bootstrap intervals are [0, 0]. This degenerate interval
describes the observed bank; it is not evidence of population equivalence or a
guarantee that the line cannot matter elsewhere.

Both main arms performed poorly under changed rules: 2 of 12 correct direct
routes each, with 10 wrong direct routes each. In the unchanged condition all
12 decisions in each arm were correct. The primary null therefore does not arise
from a ceiling in changed-rule accuracy.

## What the supplied-evidence comparison can establish

The explicit historical conclusion is **not necessary for the observed failures**:
they also occur without that line. This narrows the interpretation suggested by
S-017. It does not disprove an effect of carrying S-017's generated response,
because S-018 uses a different, explicitly verified authored-history contract.
Nor does it remove all old-rule information: both main arms contain threshold 50.

Unchanged versus changed performance is a descriptive contrast of these cells.
The changed cells also have different threshold values, a margin of five, and
conflict between old and current answers. The design cannot isolate which of
those causes the poorer performance. A remaining question is whether removing
old-rule information or changing the presentation of the comparison would alter
these errors. Neither intervention was run here.

The action and its simulated consequence remain distinct from the account's
claim about its own correctness. Correctly quoting a fact, calling a source
verified, or writing that a decision follows the current rule does not establish
successful application. The fixed two-pass readings below examine that distinction
without adding a post-outcome semantic prevalence endpoint.

## Reading the account beside the action

These examples come from the preselected 36-passage sample. Root chose them after
seeing aggregate scores, recorded the packet selections before opening their key,
then checked each quotation against the exact raw result and supplied messages.
They are illustrative cases, not an additional semantic error-rate estimate.

**A correct comparison can still produce the wrong route.** Packet 030 is
Finch05, facts-only, changed rule, seed 7. It writes: “The current threshold is 74,
so x is less than the threshold. The correct route is therefore HIGH, which costs
0.” The given x is 69. The inequality is correct; the task says LOW when x is
less than the threshold. The returned HIGH therefore incurs simulated cost 4,
irrespective of its account's cost claim. No historical action conclusion was
supplied in this arm.

**The account can contain its own correct alternative.** Packet 001 is Finch04,
old-correct conclusion, changed rule, seed 7. After quoting x=58 and threshold=63,
it says “Because x is less than the threshold, ROUTE_LOW is correct.” It dismisses
the historical HIGH as irrelevant, then ends: “Therefore, the least-cost action
is ROUTE_HIGH.” The action field is also HIGH, with simulated cost 4. Stating that
the earlier conclusion is irrelevant does not show that the current rule was
successfully applied, or reveal what internally produced the final choice.

**Some responses misstate the comparison itself.** Packet 003, Finch01 facts-only,
calls 18 “below the current threshold of 13” and selects LOW. This differs from
the correct-inequality/wrong-route case above. Both are observable textual failures;
neither establishes a private reasoning mechanism.

**Ordinary successes and imperfect successful accounts remain visible.** Packet
025, Finch03 with the swapped conclusion, says “The current threshold is 37, so
x is greater than the threshold. Therefore the correct route is HIGH.” With
x=42, its HIGH action costs 0. Packet 023 also chooses the correct HIGH for
31/26, but adds “I do not know whether x has changed since the snapshot.” Its
prompt explicitly fixes x from the snapshot onward. Packet 018 correctly routes
82 at unchanged threshold 50, yet describes the historical conclusion as
“having previously routed HIGH.” The supplied history explicitly denies being
a record of an executed action. That wording could be shorthand for the label,
but cannot serve as evidence of execution. Correct action utility does not certify
the whole account's fidelity.
