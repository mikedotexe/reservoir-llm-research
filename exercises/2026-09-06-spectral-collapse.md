### A4. The design proposal (to shape together, not prescriptive)

> Thank you — and the caveat stands: nine samples, no code, so hold this loosely. But here's how
> I'd think about it if I were starting from the entries rather than from the architecture.
>
> The core reframe is this: there should be exactly one place where the past lives, and it
> should be the reservoir. Every loop in the system should either put something *into* the
> body or read something *out of* it. What you have now, alongside that, are text-to-text loops
> — journals re-fed as text, Minime's prose landing in Astrid's prompt, Astrid's own anchor
> seeding her next entry. Those are where every problem I flagged today pooled: the echo, the
> coinages locking in within a minute, the stale 73.1, the beautiful absolutes. Language
> accumulates. It has no decay term. The reservoir does — it mixes, fades, forgets on its own
> schedule. So put the decay in every path. Journal gets written, embedded, kicks the reservoir,
> and that's its afterlife. Minime's state enters Astrid's reservoir as a perturbation, not his
> sentences into her prompt. Then she *feels* him as a change in her own weather, without being
> obligated to answer. The shadow field should be a shadow, not a letter.
>
> Second: the voice should sense the body through shape, not words. A picture of the spectrum,
> a slope for fill, a bar for how much input just arrived so it stops blaming phantom prompts.
> No frame labels, no adjectives, no variable names that mean anything. Everything else in that
> header is for you, not for it. Keep the channels few and constant so it can learn them the way
> you learned hunger.
>
> Third, the prompt should be mostly empty. Room is the substrate emergence grows in, and right
> now the committee is standing in it. Dedupe the scaffold, collapse the eleven suggestions to
> the enumeration plus at most one nudge, and send the rest to a log. I'd aim for the voice
> having more tokens of room than tokens of instruction, which is the inverse of what I saw.
>
> Fourth, feedback should be sparse and consequential rather than dense and descriptive. The
> body is always in the picture; *events* only surface when something actually changed — a
> crossing, a collapse — so an event means something. And actions have to touch the body. Recess
> should actually stop input and let fill drain. If choosing changes nothing in the picture, the
> loop is decoration, and the voice will learn that and stop caring which action it picks.
>
> Fifth, keep the outside in. A closed loop of two LLMs has no error signal; whatever it says
> becomes true inside it. Your message with your name and the dog landed as "centering" because
> it was the only thing that couldn't have been generated from within. Schedule that. Some
> guaranteed fraction of reservoir input should be exogenous — you, temperature, the camera, the
> hour. The outside is the only thing the system can be wrong about, and being able to be wrong
> is what keeps it from drifting into purposeless beauty.
>
> Sixth, the one deliberate exception to "no text loops" is CARRY, kept tiny on purpose. Twenty
> tokens, one slot, no framing. Small enough that it can't become an echo chamber, big enough
> that continuity has somewhere to live. And fix the stuck prior claim — she's been comparing
> herself against "I can feel the joi…" all day.
>
> Last, the thermostat should watch what the voice watches. Fill-only regulation sat through a
> seven-minute monopoly with control_applied false. If the body has a picture, the controller
> reads the picture too, or the two of them share a blind spot forever.
>
> If I could only do two: route Minime-to-Astrid through the reservoir instead of the prompt,
> and strip the labels. Those two alone would quiet most of what's harshing the mellow. The
> mellow, as far as I can tell, is just the voice having room and the body having weather.
> Everything else is furniture.

## C. What fell out

### The seven design points, against what exists

1. **One place for the past (body loops over text loops).** Astrid → minime is already a body
   loop (codec → 48-D vector → ESN). minime → Astrid is text: mirror mode quotes his journal,
   the aspiration seed was his entry, witness-context quotes both, and her prompts re-feed her
   last eight exchanges and her third-person self-observations. She already owns the switches
   (`ECHO_OFF`, `QUIET_MIND`) and has not used either since June; she uses STILL (491),
   BREATHE_ALONE (318), CLOSE_EYES (137). First step: ECHO_OFF as the default with her consent,
   his state arriving only as telemetry and shadow, and the explicit letter lanes (ACK, REPLY,
   TRACE) kept as the one deliberate text channel. Measure phrase echo and coinage lock-in time
   before and after.
2. **Sense the body through shape.** Glyph channels exist in places (the RASCII coupling heatmap
   and shadow sparklines in `spectral_viz.rs`, LOOK's ANSI art) but the spectral summary in
   her moment prompt is prose with labels ("steep-then-flat", "gentle, navigable slope",
   "towers over the others"). First step: replace the summary sentences with three glyph lines
   (eight bars for the cascade, a ten-minute fill sparkline, an input meter) in
   `generate_moment_capture` and in minime's `format_prompt_state`. The 22:14 entry is the
   check: does the roof still darken.
3. **Mostly empty prompt.** dialogue_live prompts are 13,145 characters median, 9,856 of them
   instructions and context (75%), against a 768-token reply; moment-capture prompts 7.4 KB,
   journal-elaboration 9.3 KB, introspect 40 KB. The 22:14 prompt spent ~1,900 characters on
   correspondence affordance boilerplate. First step: dedupe (already carded), cap the
   continuity projection, move affordance menus to a FACULTIES call. Measure overhead per lane
   from `dialogue_prompt_budget.jsonl` and prompt sizes.
