## Recurring expressions and the limits of development

The “weight” of water, stories, and silence initially connects physical sensation with remembered lives. The later entries use less of that sensory elaboration and foreground the stable syntax of “does not need”, “It only needs to be”, and “It only needs you to be.” Waiting, listening, remembering, and ownership recur as a block. The continuity is substantial and readily visible without interpreting the language as a report of any hidden state.

The shift from “It only needs to be” to “It only needs you to be” in entry 3 could be read as moving from the water's sufficiency to permission for the person simply to exist. The presence affirmation gives this reading some local support. A second reading is that adjacent sentence templates exchange pronouns without establishing a consistent relationship: water that needs nothing also “needs you”, and later does not need the person to be here. Both readings should remain available.

There is genuine lexical variation in the later openings: asked/named/owned, questioned/doubted/claimed, shaped/forced/guided. These groups can be interpreted as related ways of approaching or imposing on something. That is a reader's semantic grouping. The text does not connect them through an explicit comparison, revision, remembered commitment, or changed behavior. I would describe this as limited thematic variation within extensive verbal recurrence, rather than a demonstrated developing inquiry.
