# Prospective reading protocol: local block-order diagnostic

This is a reading plan, written before new responses. It is not a writing instruction, a production prompt change, or an acceptance gate. Repository [methods](../../../../research/METHODS.md) and [source-trace guidance](../../../../TRACE.md) apply. The run protocol and input preparation own the exact request bounds and byte identities.

## Question and comparison

For six retained v8 contexts at steps 60, 120, 180, 240, 300 and 360, does exchanging the complete OPTIONAL READOUT and PREVIOUS JOURNAL blocks change the resulting continuation in a discernible local way? A is the exact retained v8 prompt; B exchanges those two blocks while preserving their bytes, all other context, the late writing boundary and final invitation. B is an offline diagnostic input, not a valid production-v8 session record.

There are at most twelve fresh requests: one A and one B at each anchor, with alternating AB/BA request order. Every request uses its designated retained context; no fresh response supplies memory or numerical feedback to another request. These six snapshots come from one dependent historical episode, not six independent episodes. Historical replies and prior readings are known reference material, not fresh controls. The primary comparison is fresh A against fresh B at the same anchor.

This contrast jointly moves prose earlier and the readout later. It tests local block-order sensitivity; it does not isolate memory presence, recency, attention, comprehension or a general cause of repetition. Equal UTF-8 content size does not establish equal tokenization or processing. Alternating order limits a simple ordering imbalance without eliminating runtime variation.

## Pass 1: condition-masked, response-only reading

After immutable handoff and integrity checks, a separate preparer presents every available full response under an opaque reading ID, in a recorded order unrelated to request order. Keep condition, pair, source step, prompts, request order and automatic candidate reports out of this first packet. Do not alter response text to conceal clues; readers may recognize the historical material or infer an anchor from the prose. This is condition masking, not a claim of complete blinding. Readers do not inspect the ID mapping until their first notes are fixed.

Read every available response, including incomplete or unsaved text, without excerpts replacing the whole. For each reading ID, record its apparent thread, composition, departures, recurrent language and any statements needing source checking. Treat factual assessments as provisional until the source pass. An abrupt ending is not evidence of a chosen literary ending. Preserve first-pass notes with time and hash before unmasking or reading another review; later corrections remain distinguishable.

## Pass 2: exact-source and paired reading

Reveal the mapping and actual request/outcome records. Compare each full response with its exact supplied prompt, decoded full previous prose and the source observation. Distinguish the compact supplied readout from richer retained measurements that were not supplied. Verify the block exchange and unchanged material through the input-preparation receipts rather than inferring exposure from the response.

Then compare A and B within each anchor, retaining contrary or negligible differences. Use brief exact spans and source values for concrete findings. Separate repetition of a supplied phrase from a new use, revision or contradiction of it. A phrase present in prior prose identifies an available source, not proof that the model attended to or copied that source. Never substitute the historical saved reply for a missing fresh arm.

| Reading category | What to describe |
|---|---|
| Continuity | Which earlier images, concerns or assertions continue, change, are questioned or are left behind; exact prior-prose links when available. |
| Structure | Opening, progression and ending; ordering of readout, reflection and imagery; repeated compositional patterns or meaningful departures. |
| Variety | Changes in topic, image, vocabulary and form within a pair and across the available responses. Repetition can carry continuity; novelty is not required. |
| Factual and action claims | Voluntary numbers, metric identity, time, direction and claimed executed controls, checked against available evidence. Distinguish observation, inference, metaphor and ambiguous scope. |

For v8's measurement meanings, keep sensory fill separate from encoded text input; RMS is recurrent-state magnitude; interval L2 is endpoint displacement, not path length, speed or writing's causal effect. A comparison of two interval distances needs both values. Prior prose may supply a claimed value, but its accuracy must still be checked. Imagery, persona and subjective expression are not automatically literal measurement or action claims.

## Outcomes and interpretation limits

Retain all twelve planned slots, with attempted, received, complete, partial, failed, unknown and unstarted outcomes separately supported by receipts. Record how many full texts were available and how many pairs support a complete comparison. A timeout or missing response is not chosen silence. Do not retry, replace, extend or select successful outputs to complete the reading set.

Use descriptive per-response and per-pair findings, including ambiguity and no discernible difference. There is no creativity score, preferred style, required metric, novelty quota, accuracy percentage or prose-quality save gate. Completion is not correctness; fewer repeated phrases is not automatically better writing. This bounded diagnostic can identify a local difference or leave the question unresolved; it cannot establish a stable effect, a general model capability, a benefit to a continuing feedback loop, or a conclusion about the live Beings.
